# Split the remote into a crisp face and a dark, curved back

The umote enclosure will look and feel premium if it copies the 2021 Siri Remote's profile, a flat face with crisp edges over a rounded back, and uses the parting line between the two printed shells as a deliberate low shadow line rather than hiding it. In practice that means four parts. A front shell (about 4-5 mm tall) is printed face-down on textured PEI. A darker back shell carries the palm swell and the large back radii. A raised ring accent and PETG button caps complete the set. Every downward-facing surface is chamfered at 45 degrees so nothing needs support. The overall proportions are already right: at 206 x 51 mm the remote is almost exactly as long relative to its width as the Sofabaton X2, a commercial remote with the same 2.4-inch touchscreen. What is wrong is how thick it looks. A 15.8 mm vertical side wall reads chunky, so the design has to hide most of that depth under a curved, receding back. Ergonomically, the D-pad at 108 mm sits where the thumb naturally lands and the estimated centre of mass falls just below it, which is good. The touchscreen at 10-60 mm is out of one-handed thumb reach, though, so it should work as a display first. The 8.6 mm keys are slightly under thumb-target guidance and need a 13 mm pitch and tactile differentiation. Most of the numbers below are design heuristics derived from cited principles. No source publishes fillet radii or reveal widths for commercial remotes.

## The umote's proportions already match the best touchscreen remote; only its thickness reads wrong

Acclaimed remotes cluster at **33-54 mm wide, 3.3:1 to 4.3:1 long-to-wide, and 9-20 mm thick**. The umote is 4.04:1, which lands between the **Sofabaton X2 (190 x 48 x 13 mm, 2.4-inch touchscreen, metal body)** ([manuals.plus](https://manuals.plus/asin/B0FSZRZD6X); [Digitec](https://www.digitec.ch/en/s1/product/sofabaton-x2-universal-remote-control-bluetooth-infrared-wi-fi-remote-controls-66656812)) and the **Siri Remote (136 x 35 x 9.25 mm)** ([Bechtle](https://bechtle.com/de-en/shop/apple-siri-remote-2nd-gen--4548876--p)). The weak ratio is width to thickness. At 3.23 the umote is chunkier than the Siri Remote (3.8) or the Sofabaton (3.7), though slimmer than the Google TV Streamer remote (137 x 38 x 16 mm) ([Google Store](https://store.google.com/gb/product/google_tv_streamer_remote)) and the Samsung SolarCell (163.6 x 38.6 x 15.7 mm) ([PC Richard](https://www.pcrichard.com/samsung-solarcell-remote-for-tvs/VG-TM2180ES.html)). Those thicker remotes get away with their depth through pill-shaped or curved backs. The lesson is that 15.8 mm is acceptable as long as it never shows as one vertical wall.

Designers agree that thickness is read at the edge. "Chamfer the edges. Thickness is much more apparent on the edge of a surface than it is in the middle," and a dark rear "recedes into shadow" while a lighter front plate carries the visual identity ([Core77](https://boards.core77.com/t/how-to-create-flat-sleek-design/15005)). A 45-degree chamfer defines an edge with a shadow line rather than visual bulk ([edge profile guide](https://mebelipoporuchka.pages.dev/blog/2026-03-10-edge-profiles-custom-furniture-guide)). Apple's 2021 remote puts this into practice. Its "sharp aluminum edges and a curved back" replaced a "pancake-flat" predecessor ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2021/05/24/second-generation-siri-remote-review/)), and AppleInsider found the combination grippier ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/inside/siri-remote)). For the umote, that means a flat top face with a fine edge break, a visible straight band of about 6-8 mm, and then a large R6-8 back radius curving under into shadow. Put the colour break about 4-5 mm below the face, so the light top plate reads as a 5 mm sheet floating on a dark body.

Colour, material and finish (CMF) follow one pattern across premium remotes: **one body material or colour, with at most one accent**. Google matches each remote to its streamer in a single soft colour with tone-on-tone buttons ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/google-nest/nest-thermostat-google-tv-streamer-design-details)). B&O's Halo ring is the one element that "appears to float" ([Malibu Wired](https://av.malibuwired.com/en/remotes/beoremote-halo/)). The Siri Remote makes its raised directional ring the hero control ([TidBITS](https://tidbits.com/2021/05/24/second-generation-siri-remote-review/)). The umote's 35 mm ring D-pad is the natural accent. Teenage Engineering's "simple geometry, basic color palette" ([SFMOMA](https://www.sfmoma.org/read/stay-curious-stay-naive-an-interview-with-teenage-engineering-jesper-kouthoofd/)) is the antidote to the generic DIY tells: unequal margins, mismatched radii, many key sizes and over-labelling. Frame the screen with a flush black bezel whose window corners are concentric with the body corners, not with a raised frame or a deep well. The remote's long length is an asset. B&O's 243 mm Beoremote One shows that extreme slenderness reads as luxury when the face is clean ([Beoworld](https://archived.beoworld.org/prod_details.asp?pid=1351)), so leave empty space at the bottom rather than spreading the keys out to fill it.

## Thumb reach puts the D-pad right and the touchscreen out of range

Hand sizes for the design range run from **165 mm (5th-percentile female) to 210 mm (95th-percentile male) hand length** ([Made Good Designs, ANSUR II](https://madegooddesigns.com/tools/hand-size-chart/)). A 51 mm width is narrower than any phone tested in the width studies, where comfort and thumb performance degrade as width grows ([Lee et al. 2016](https://scholarworks.unist.ac.kr/handle/201301/18907); [Applied Ergonomics 2018](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_miscellaneous_1963269489)), so width is not a risk. Do not go wider, though. The most comfortable firm-grip diameter is 19.7% of hand length ([Made Good Designs, citing Kong & Lowe 2005](https://madegooddesigns.com/tools/hand-size-chart/)), which is a circumference of about 102-130 mm. With edge radii, the umote's cross-section perimeter is already about 125-135 mm. That makes **18.8 mm (15.8 plus the 3 mm swell) a hard ceiling** on section depth.

Measured mean thumb lengths are about 56-66 mm ([MATEC 2016](https://www.matec-conferences.org/articles/matecconf/pdf/2016/03/matecconf_icmes2016_09001.pdf)). By my own geometry, if the thumb lands on OK at y = 108 mm, the comfortable band runs roughly from y = 80 to y = 140, and a large hand can stretch to about y = 65-70 without regripping. Hoober's field data show users shift their grip constantly, and that the centre of the reachable area is touched fastest and most accurately ([UXmatters](http://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php)). Logitech put the Harmony Elite's screen at the top precisely to avoid accidental touches ([AnandTech](https://at-web1.www.anandtech.com/show/11125/the-logitech-harmony-elite-experience-ultimate-control/2)). This is a good trade, but it means the umote's screen (y = 10-60) is a glance-and-occasionally-tap surface. It needs a smooth, low-friction back below it so the hand can slide up, and any targets meant for one-handed use belong in its bottom third. Volume, Back and Home belong at y = 130-150, where the thumb only has to flex slightly. Rarely used keys go lowest.

Balance is favourable. Assuming a 55 g LiPo centred near y = 148, a 15 g display at y = 35 and an 80 g shell and PCB near y = 103, the **centre of mass lands at about 113 mm**, just below the D-pad and inside the grip zone of y = 100-170. The remote will not nose forward when the thumb stretches toward the screen. This is my estimate; no published study of remote centre-of-mass preference exists. The research suggests heft reads as quality. Reviewers praise the "great button feel and weight" of the 63 g Siri Remote ([AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/inside/siri-remote)), and owners call the roughly 109 g Sofabaton X1S "beefy, solid, stable in your hand" ([AV Nirvana](https://avnirvana.com/threads/real-world-review-the-sofabaton-x1s-smart-remote.14324)). Even so, aim toward 150 g, and keep the battery low.

Apple prototyped a shallow oval finger-rest indent on the Siri Remote's back, then shipped it smooth ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2022/10/03/2021-siri-remote-finger-rest)). Deep grooves fight the regripping Hoober observed. The palm swell should sit over the battery at y = 110-180 and blend over a long radius into the thinner screen end. That also makes the remote asymmetric end to end, which fixes the "held upside down" problem the original Siri Remote had ([Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/347504/2021-siri-remote-review-apple-tv.html)). An optional index-finger locator should be no deeper than 1-1.5 mm, behind y = 95-115. Because the back is curved, the remote needs a flat land or feet at least 30 mm apart, including one near the tip, so that pressing the screen while the remote lies on a table does not rock it.

Thumb-target research recommends **9.2-9.6 mm** for one-handed use ([Parhi et al. 2006](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=155150)), and NRC guidance gives 10 mm for fingertip pushbuttons ([NRC](https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1909/ML19092A160.pdf)). The 8.6 mm keys are workable as physical keys with edges and travel, but only with a **12-13 mm pitch**. Three columns at 13 mm span 34.6 mm, which leaves about 8 mm side margins on a 51 mm body. MIL-STD-1472 calls for concave or high-friction button surfaces ([Design Data Digest](https://www.scribd.com/document/328391877/Human-Engineering-Design-Data-Digest)). Sofabaton owners complain that volume "blends in" with the other keys ([AV Nirvana](https://avnirvana.com/threads/real-world-review-the-sofabaton-x1s-smart-remote.14324)). Differentiate volume by shape or by a nub, give the D-pad a raised rim around a flush or concave OK, and vary cap profile by group.

## A face-down front shell and a lipped back make the seam look moulded

The core FDM rule is that every visible surface either sits on the bed or faces up, and the seam is an overlapping lip, never a butt joint. A flush butt joint between two FDM parts always shows mismatch, and around a screen or LED it "almost always leak[s] light" ([QIDI](https://qidi3d.com/blogs/print-lab/fix-light-leaks-gaps-3d-printed-lamps)). The only fully dimensioned recipe found comes from the Makers Making Change OpenAT guide, tuned on a Bambu P1S and a Prusa MK4. It specifies **1.6 mm walls, a 1.6 mm tall lip in the band 0.2-1.8 mm from the inner wall, a 1.8 mm chamfer under the lip so it prints unsupported, and a 0.4 mm chamfer on the outer edge of both halves** "so any misalignment doesn't create a visible overhang" ([MMC Shadow Lines](https://makersmakingchange.github.io/OpenAT_Design_Guide/Design_Elements_For_3D_Printed_Parts/Shadow-Lines.html)). The lip also self-aligns the halves and stiffens the shell ([Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2023/08/07/enhance-your-enclosures-with-a-shadow-line)). The two 0.4 mm chamfers form a V-reveal about 0.8 mm wide, roughly two extrusion widths. Place it 4-5 mm below the top face and it doubles as the colour break described above.

Orientation decides surface quality. Bed-facing surfaces print smoothest ([Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)), and textured PEI gives an even, injection-moulded stipple that hides layer lines ([evetech](https://evezone.evetech.co.za/deep-dives/textured-pei-vs-smooth-pei-build-plates-which-finish-for-which-print)). So print the front shell face-down. The screen window and key holes then become plain vertical through-cuts with bed-crisp edges, and the side walls carry fine lengthwise layer lines if the 206 mm axis lies along X or Y. That length fits 256 mm beds (Bambu P1/X1/A1) and the 250 x 210 mm Prusa MK4 bed, but not a 180 mm A1 mini except diagonally. The back shell has to be printed outside-up, with the swell built from layers. Variable layer height (about 0.08 mm on the shallow crown, up to 0.2 mm on the steep flanks) suppresses stair-stepping, though PrusaSlicer's own KB says it only "partly" improves curves ([Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)). Ironing does nothing on slopes. Put the Z-seam on an inside face, as controller-shell designers do ([Printables 8BitDo grip](https://www.printables.com/model/355248-8bitdo-zero-2-grip)), or use OrcaSlicer's scarf seams ([OrcaSlicer wiki](https://www.orcaslicer.com/wiki/print_settings/quality/quality_settings_seam)). Print buttons at 0.12-0.16 mm, visible face up ([Held Games](https://heldgames.com/guides/how-to-3d-print-handheld-shell-buttons)).

Printing without supports comes down to a few limits. Keep overhangs within **45-50 degrees from vertical** ([Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135); [Hydra Research](https://www.hydraresearch3d.com/design-rules)). Use chamfers, not fillets, on downward edges. Keep bridges under about 10 mm, give horizontal holes teardrop roofs ([Pollen AM](https://www.pollen.am/design_for_3d_printing_holes)), and add a **0.3-0.5 mm chamfer on every bed edge** to absorb elephant's foot ([Hydra Research](https://www.hydraresearch3d.com/design-rules)). The design consequence for the umote is that the display ledge spans the full 51 mm width, far too long to bridge, so it must be a 45-degree chamfered step. The same goes for PCB ledges and the lip underside.

For joining, the back holds bosses with **M2 x 3 heat-set inserts in a 3.2 mm hole at least 4 mm deep, with a 6 mm boss tied to the wall by ribs** ([CNC Kitchen datasheet](https://www.cnckitchen.com/s/CNCKitchen_Heat-Set-Insert-Dimensions-and-Design-Guidelines.pdf)). Install them at about 225 C for PLA and 245 C for PETG ([CNC Kitchen](https://www.cnckitchen.com/blog/tipps-amp-tricks-fr-gewindeeinstze-im-3d-druck-3awey)). Hide the screws under a rubber foot strip or a battery-door plate. Visible face screws read as DIY unless they are deliberate graphics, as at Nothing ([Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/design/product-design/why-nothing-designs-its-phones-like-logos)). A hooked tongue at the tip with two screws at the butt halves the screw count. That is common commercial practice, but no source in the research documents it. If snaps are used instead, size them with ε = 1.5·t·y/L² ([Fictiv](https://www.fictiv.com/articles/how-to-design-snap-fit-components)) at no more than 2% strain in PLA. That gives hooks 8-10 mm long, 1.2 mm thick tapering to 0.8 mm, with a 0.6 mm undercut, built in the XY plane, because Z-built beams lose about half their elongation ([Core77](https://www.core77.com/posts/65318/How-to-Design-Snap-Fit-Components)). Use PETG rather than PLA for snaps ([Hubs](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing/)). Clearances on a tuned Bambu or Prusa are **0.1 mm for tight fits** ([MMC](https://makersmakingchange.github.io/OpenAT_Design_Guide/3D_Printing_Design_Tips/3D-Printing-Design-Tips.html)) and 0.2-0.3 mm for sliding parts such as button caps ([Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)).

Stiffness comes from closing the box. Walls of four perimeters (about 1.8 mm) ([Prusa KB](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/modeling-with-3d-printing-in-mind_164135)), the perimeter lip, one or two 1.2 mm transverse bulkheads that also locate the PCB, and four fastening points make a torsionally stiff shell. Moulding's 60% rib-thickness limit exists to avoid sink marks ([Protolabs](https://www.protolabs.com/resources/design-tips/design-stronger-molded-parts/)), and FDM has no sink marks, so that limit does not apply. For finish, matte PLA or PETG on textured PEI is the no-paint premium route ([Bambu forum](https://forum.bambulab.com/t/matte-optics-for-petg-vs-pla/172750)). The paint route is wet-sanding from 220 to 2000 grit, then filler primer, then a satin clear coat ([JLC3DP](https://jlc3dp.com/blog/pla-smoothing)). PLA softens at about 55-60 C, which makes PETG the safer body material for a remote that might be left in a hot car or a sunny window.

## Numbered design rules and recommended dimensions

The rules below combine the sourced limits above with geometry worked out for this remote. The y values are measured from the tip.

**Form and aesthetics**

1. Keep the 206 x 51 mm plan. Use a plan-view corner radius of R10-12, with every other radius derived from it.
2. Make the top face flat. Break its edge with a 0.6-1.0 mm chamfer or a 0.5-1 mm radius, the crisp highlight line.
3. Keep the visible straight side band at 6-8 mm. Below it, roll the back into an R6-8 radius so the full 15.8 mm never shows as a wall.
4. Place the parting line and colour break 4-5 mm below the face. Make the front plate light or mid-tone and the back shell one step darker.
5. Use one body colour plus one accent only, on the D-pad ring or the power key. Keep keys tone-on-tone.
6. Use a flush, black, single-colour screen bezel. Make the window corner radius concentric with the body (body R minus the offset). Use no raised frame and no well deeper than the panel stack.
7. Keep one margin number of about 6-8 mm for the screen side bezel, the key-grid side margin and the ring margin. Centre everything on the long axis.
8. Leave the area below about y = 170 clear of keys. That empty space is a feature, not wasted area.

**Ergonomics**

9. Ring D-pad: 35 mm outer diameter centred at y = 108. Raise the rim about 0.5-0.8 mm above the face, and set OK flush or 0.3 mm concave.
10. Key grid: 3 x 3 at a **13 mm pitch** both ways. With the first row centred at about y = 138, the rows sit at y = 138, 151 and 164, and the last key edge is at about y = 168. Side margins come out at about 8 mm.
11. Put Back, Home and Volume in the top row (y of about 138). Give Volume a distinct cap or a 0.5 mm nub on "+". Put a 0.5 mm home nub on the single most-used key.
12. Make primary caps concave (0.3-0.5 mm dish) and secondary caps flat. Keys protrude 1.0-1.5 mm above the face.
13. Palm swell: a maximum of +3 mm, centred at y = 130-160, spanning y = 110-180, and blended over 30 mm or more into the screen end. Total section depth must not exceed 18.8 mm.
14. Optional index locator: 1-1.5 mm deep, behind y = 95-115. No deeper grooves, because the hand must be able to slide up to the screen.
15. Table stability: a flat land or two feet at least 30 mm apart under the swell, plus a small foot near y = 25-40 so screen presses don't rock the remote.
16. Keep the LiPo at y = 95-200 and the target mass at 150 g or less, so the centre of mass stays at y of about 110-115.
17. Treat the screen as glance-first. Put touch targets of at least 10 mm in its lower third, and make everything D-pad-navigable.

**FDM construction**

18. Four parts: the front shell (face-down, textured PEI), the back shell with swell (outside up, variable layers 0.08-0.2 mm), the ring accent (face-down, separate colour, press-fit with 0.1 mm clearance), and PETG button caps (visible face up, 0.12-0.16 mm layers).
19. Walls 1.6-1.8 mm. Top skin 1.6 mm. Two 1.2 mm transverse bulkheads that also locate the PCB.
20. Shadow-line lip: 1.6 mm tall, in the band 0.2-1.8 mm from the inner wall, with a 1.8 mm 45-degree chamfer underneath, a 0.4 mm outer chamfer on both halves (about a 0.8 mm V-reveal), and 0.1 mm clearance.
21. Every downward-facing surface within 45 degrees of vertical. Use chamfers, never fillets, facing the bed. Bridges under 10 mm. The display ledge becomes a 45-degree chamfered step. Teardrop any horizontal hole.
22. 0.4-0.5 mm chamfer on all bed-contact edges. Slicer elephant-foot compensation at 0-0.15 mm so key holes stay on size.
23. Fasteners: four M2 x 3 heat-set inserts in the back (3.2 mm hole, at least 4 mm deep, 6 mm boss ribbed to the wall), with M2 x 6-8 screws hidden under a foot strip. Alternatively, a tip hook plus two screws at the butt.
24. Button caps with 0.2-0.3 mm radial clearance per side and a retaining flange 0.3-0.5 mm larger than the hole.
25. Z-seam on inner faces or scarfed. Run the 206 mm axis along X or Y. Check the bed holds 206 mm (the A1 mini does not).
26. Material: matte PETG for the body (heat resistance) or matte PLA for easiest printing. No silk filament on structural parts. If the remote will be painted: wet-sand to 2000 grit, filler primer, then a satin 2K clear coat.

|---|---|
| Plan corner radius | R10-12 |
| Top edge break | 0.6-1.0 mm chamfer |
| Visible side band | 6-8 mm |
| Back edge radius | R6-8 |
| Parting line below face | 4-5 mm |
| Reveal (V-groove) | about 0.8 wide x 0.4 deep |
| Palm swell | +3 mm max, y 110-180 |
| D-pad | 35 mm OD at y = 108, rim +0.5-0.8 mm |

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing is that the parting line is not a defect to minimise. It is the main styling tool. On an FDM remote, the seam, the colour break and the "thin plate on a dark body" illusion can all be the same 0.8 mm reveal 4-5 mm below the face. One design decision then handles print orientation (front face on the bed), visual thickness (the back recedes) and assembly tolerance (the lip self-aligns). The same logic means the palm swell and back curvature should live entirely in the back shell, the one part that has to be printed with layer lines on show. It is also the part that carries the darker colour, where layer lines show least.

The open risk is the touchscreen. No layout change brings y = 10-60 mm into one-handed thumb reach on a 206 mm body. The real choice is between accepting a regrip, with a slide-friendly back and D-pad navigation of the UI, and shortening the remote, which would cost battery length. Several numbers here are derived, not measured: the radii, the reveal size, the swell placement and the centre-of-mass estimate. Before the final print, test them with a quick hand trial on a printed back shell and a printed key-pitch coupon.
